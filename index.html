Methodology and Sources

What the score measures

The score is a working risk model, 0 to 100, higher is worse. For the Medicare-certified track, it answers one question: how much operational risk do your agency's own numbers suggest, based on four Medicare-certified home health quality measures?

InputWeightHow it scores
CMS Quality of Patient Care star rating15 ptsEach star below 5 adds 15 points
Hospital readmission rate (percent)2 ptsEach percentage point adds 2 points
Patient satisfaction (percent)1.2 ptsEach point below 100 adds 1.2 points
OASIS documentation on-time rate (percent)1.1 ptsEach point below 100 adds 1.1 points

The points are added, rounded to a whole number, and capped at 100. Risk bands: under 50 is Low, 50 to 74 is Moderate, 75 and up is High.

The non-medical home care track

The non-medical score answers the same question for personal care agencies, using the four operating numbers that drive risk in that world:

InputWeightHow it scores
Caregiver turnover rate (percent)0.4 ptsEach percentage point adds 0.4 points
Unfilled shifts (percent)1.5 ptsEach percentage point adds 1.5 points
EVV or documentation exceptions (percent)1.2 ptsEach percentage point adds 1.2 points
Authorized hours not delivered (percent)0.7 ptsEach point below 100 percent delivered adds 0.7 points

Same bands: under 50 is Low, 50 to 74 is Moderate, 75 and up is High. Speed to first visit, supervisory visits, care plan reviews, and referral source (questions 5 to 8) never change the numeric score. They only decide which recommendations you see, and their thresholds are working assumptions, not validated cutoffs.

What does not affect the score

Turnover, start-of-care delay, documentation lag, and referral source (questions 5 to 8) never change the numeric score. They only decide which recommendations you see. The flag thresholds are working assumptions, not validated cutoffs:

What the score does and does not mean

It does: give you a structured read on your own numbers, flag the biggest drivers, and point you at concrete next steps.

It does not: benchmark you against other agencies, predict revenue or reimbursement, diagnose compliance problems, or replace an operational audit. There is no CMS data, no national benchmark, and no external dataset behind it. Every input is self-reported by you.

Medicare-certified measures vs. home care links

The four scored inputs come from the Medicare-certified home health world: CMS star ratings, HHCAHPS satisfaction, and OASIS are Medicare program measures. Two linked toolkits, the Caregiver Pay and Retention Playbook and the Private-Pay Client Playbook, are written for non-medical home care (caregiver teams and private-pay clients). Each link states its scope so the two worlds are never confused.

Limits

  • All inputs are self-reported. The tool cannot verify them.
  • The weights and thresholds reflect the author's professional judgment from years running Boswell Consulting Agency and working inside home health operations. They are not validated research, and this tool does not draw on the author's doctoral research.
  • Not a CMS-validated instrument. Not clinical, legal, or financial advice.
  • Correlation is not causation: a high score means your numbers look risky, not that any specific outcome will happen.

Sources

  • Federal rules require OASIS assessments to be transmitted within 30 days of the assessment (42 CFR 484.45).
  • CMS publishes Quality of Patient Care Star Ratings for home health agencies on Care Compare.
  • For Medicaid-funded personal care services, federal law requires electronic visit verification that captures the type of service, the individual receiving it, the date of service, the location, the individual providing the service, and the start and end times (Section 12006 of the 21st Century Cures Act, 42 USC 1396b(l)).
  • Everything else on this page, including every weight, threshold, and interpretation, is the author's professional judgment. No national benchmarks, no CMS datasets, and no third-party studies are used.
