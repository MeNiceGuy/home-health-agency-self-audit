What most owners miss

The replacement number comes first

Growth math that skips losses overstates progress. An owner who signs four clients in a month and loses three has grown by one, and the marketing report still says four. This calculator puts the drip on the page first because the drip is the part nobody budgets for. Before your agency grows by a single client, it has to replace the clients the month already took.

You market for inquiries, not for clients

No ad, referral partner, or discharge planner hands you a client. They hand you an inquiry, and your intake process turns some share of those into clients. That share is your close rate, and it is a marketing number: at a 25 percent close rate, four inquiries make one client. Raise the close rate and the inquiry target falls with it, which is why intake speed and follow-up discipline belong in the marketing plan and not the admin pile.

Length of stay is a growth lever

The drip is your census divided by your average stay. On a 50 client census, moving the average stay from 24 months to 30 months cuts the monthly losses from about 2.1 clients to about 1.7. That is about one less client to replace every two to three months, bought with service consistency instead of ad spend. Retention work is acquisition work at a lower price.
