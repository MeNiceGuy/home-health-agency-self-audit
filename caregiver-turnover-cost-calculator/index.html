What moves this number

First, find out where your caregivers leave. Track departures by how long the caregiver had been with you. If most exits land in the first 90 days, the fix is the structure of those 90 days: a named trainer, a check-in at week one, thirty, sixty, and ninety, and a schedule that does not change without warning.

Second, shorten the uncovered weeks. A small bench of on-call caregivers who already cleared your screening costs little to hold and cuts the margin you lose every time someone walks.

Third, recheck this number quarterly. Turnover cost compounds quietly, and the quarter you catch it early is the cheapest one to fix.

Methodology and sources

The calculator prices three things for every departure: what you spend to replace the caregiver (recruiting and screening, paid orientation and training hours, and the hiring time of you or your administrator), and the margin on the hours that sit uncovered while the seat is empty (weeks uncovered, times weekly hours, times your bill rate minus caregiver pay). Multiply by the number of caregivers who left and you have the year.
